## Economic Role

The commodity-trade module connects producers and consumers across locations. It determines delivered prices, bilateral expenditure shares, domestic trade, border effects, and the market-access consequences of geography and policy.

## Baseline Specification

The baseline applies iceberg trade costs to differentiated varieties under CES demand. A fraction of shipped output melts in transit, so the destination price equals the origin price multiplied by the bilateral friction.

## Choosing a Specification

Use CES gravity for differentiated-variety models, Eaton–Kortum for Ricardian sourcing, explicit networks for infrastructure placement, and nontraded goods when local-service demand is quantitatively important.

## Literature Map

Dekle, Eaton, and Kortum (2008) develops exact-change counterfactuals around gravity. Allen and Arkolakis (2014) embeds trade and mobility in a spatial economy, while transport-network applications make routes and infrastructure explicit.
