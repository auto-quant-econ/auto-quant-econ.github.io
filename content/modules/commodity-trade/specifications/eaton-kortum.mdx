## Definition

Each country draws productivity across a continuum of goods. Buyers purchase each good from the origin with the lowest production cost inclusive of bilateral trade frictions.

## Core Equations

The probability that an origin supplies a destination becomes its bilateral expenditure share. Relative-change formulas update these shares using baseline trade shares, changes in unit costs, and a trade elasticity determined by productivity dispersion.

## Economic Intuition

High-productivity origins capture more markets, while bilateral barriers reduce sourcing. A larger trade elasticity makes expenditure shares more sensitive to delivered-cost changes.

## Literature Lineage

Eaton and Kortum (2002) develop the Ricardian sourcing model. Dekle, Eaton, and Kortum (2008) show how its gravity structure supports counterfactual analysis in changes around an observed equilibrium.
