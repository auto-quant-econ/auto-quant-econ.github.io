## Economic Role

The preferences module turns prices, income, housing, and local attributes into household demand and welfare. It determines what consumers buy, how the cost of living is measured, and how nominal income is translated into real utility across locations.

## Baseline Specification

The baseline uses a constant-elasticity-of-substitution aggregator over differentiated varieties. Housing enters alongside traded consumption, amenities are exogenous, and households are homogeneous. The resulting demand system delivers bilateral expenditure shares and a consumption price index.

## Choosing a Specification

Use the one-sector CES baseline when the research question centers on market access and aggregate welfare. Add sectors for structural transformation or sector-specific shocks; nonhomotheticity for income-dependent demand; endogenous amenities for congestion or local public goods; and quality choice when product upgrading is central.

## Literature Map

The CES and gravity foundations connect this family to modern trade and spatial models. Multisector applications include quantitative trade models with input–output structure, while urban and spatial work develops endogenous amenity and housing formulations.
